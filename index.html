01 · About

Where operations, money and mobility meet.

I'm an operations leader with more than ten years of experience growing businesses across West, East and North Africa and the wider EMEA region. My work sits where mobility, fintech and day-to-day operations overlap: getting vehicles and vehicle financing to the people who need them, keeping banks and foreign partners confident, and building the processes that let a business grow without falling apart.

I've launched operations in new markets, grown loan portfolios, cut default rates, and led teams across Product, Risk, Compliance and Customer Experience. Today I head import operations at Autochek Africa, coordinating partners on two continents. 02 · Now

Moving cars across an ocean and into six African markets.

As Head of Import Operations at Autochek Africa, I own the whole journey of a vehicle: from a customer's request, to an auction lot in the United States, Canada, Japan, South Korea, China or the Middle East, onto a ship, and into port in Nigeria, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Kenya or Uganda.

Platform · Product design

Import Hub

One system for every vehicle import. A back office where Import Associates, Sourcing, Finance and Ops work each import from one record; a customer portal to choose vehicles, pay and download statements; and a public page to track a car by code or VIN.

  • 15 tracked steps
  • 6 markets
  • Access set per market
  • English · Français · العربية

Planning & intelligence

Portwise Pro

A workspace for planning and running vehicle shipments to Africa. It compares routes and costs from seven source countries, shows sailing schedules, tracks shipments, and brings market rules, carrier advisories and a holiday calendar into one place, alongside carrier quotes, invoicing and a dashboard of how the team uses it.

  • Route planner
  • Sailing schedules
  • Shipment tracking
  • Market guide
  • News & advisories
  • Carrier quotes
  • Invoicing
  • Team usage

Self-service

Import Help Centre

Customers answer their own questions: the step-by-step import journey, a delivery-time planner, an eligibility check (steering, age, title), market-by-market rules and a plain-English glossary of shipping and customs terms.

Weekly shipping newsletter

Dockside Weekly

A weekly shipping newsletter that turns shipping news into decisions for our customers. Each issue gives the status of every lane into Lagos (USA, Korea, Japan, China, the UAE), flags disruptions and deadlines, checks port congestion and the exchange rate, and ends with what to do before the next bid. Every figure is sourced.

08 · Background

A physicist who ended up in operations.

I studied Physics and Electronics at the Federal University of Technology, Akure. That training taught me to look at how a system behaves, where it breaks, and why. I still work that way.

I started out keeping networks and servers running at Computer Warehouse Group, moved into telematics where hardware, data and customers meet, and from there into mobility, finance and now cross-border trade. Most of my work in recent years has been about helping drivers and customers get the vehicles they need, and making sure the money behind that works for everyone involved.

Outside work, I'm drawn to the same questions: how cities move, how data can be shown so people actually use it, and what infrastructure emerging markets need next.

09 · How I work

Four habits behind the numbers.

  1. Start with the data

    Before I change anything I build the dashboard that shows what's actually happening. Most of my wins started as a chart someone hadn't seen yet.

  2. Process before scale

    Playbooks, SOPs and clean reconciliation come first. They're how one market becomes six without the quality dropping.

  3. Partners are relationships

    Banks, platforms and overseas partners stay when they trust you. I'm open with them, I bring the numbers, and I do what I said I'd do.

  4. Teams over heroes

    The results here came from Product, Risk, Compliance, CX and field teams pulling in one direction. My job is to make that easy.
